How to read the network

|

Nodes (actors)

Helix

Helix - the primary sphere the actor belongs to:

  • Government: public sector bodies, agencies, ministries
  • Industry: private companies, startups, corporations
  • Academia: universities, research institutes, labs
  • Intermediary: span or connect other helices (accelerators, foundations)
  • Civil Society: NGOs, advocacy groups, professional associations
  • Unknown: helix couldn't be determined from available sources
Sphere

Sphere - how many helices an actor participates in:

  • Single: primarily embedded in one helix across mandate, governance, and function
  • Multi: Intermediary category, spans multiple helices in mandate, governance, or function
R&D

R&D - whether the actor engages in research activity:

  • R&D: basic research, applied research, or an explicit innovation mandate indicated
  • Assessed: reviewed but no clear indication of research activity when entity mentioned

Edges (interactions)

Label

Label - the type of interaction between two actors:

  • Technology Transfer: core activity of innovation systems, market or non-market transfer of technology, knowledge, or IP
  • Collaborative Leadership: an innovation organiser exercises convening power to bring spheres together, coordinating top-down and bottom-up processes
  • Substitution: one sphere fills gaps when another is weak, one actor takes the role of another
  • Networking: formal or informal networks, a manifestation of the collective nature of science, technology and innovation
  • Collaboration Conflict Moderation: triadic moderation of tensions and conflicts, turning diverging interests into convergence and win-win outcomes
Functional space

Functional space - determined by the helix pair of the two actors:

  • Knowledge: Academia ↔ Government
  • Innovation: Academia ↔ Industry | Academia ↔ Intermediary | Industry ↔ Intermediary
  • Consensus: Government ↔ Industry | Government ↔ Intermediary
  • Public: any pair involving Civil Society
  • Intra-Helix: same helix on both
